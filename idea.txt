YES. And I think the idea becomes way more interesting if you don’t build “a mentalism learning app.”

Build a personal transformation system where mentalism is one of the major skill trees.

The fantasy is:

“Become the person who walks into a room, notices everything, understands people, stays calm under pressure, communicates exceptionally well, and makes better decisions.”

Patrick Jane is the aesthetic/reference point — not the literal goal.

I looked at what BeFreed and Deepstash are doing today. BeFreed is particularly relevant because it combines personalized learning paths, source material, AI-generated audio lessons, practice with feedback, notes, and progress based on real-world growth.  Deepstash’s strongest idea is its bite-sized idea-card + personalized feed + personal knowledge library model. 

Your app could combine those ideas with actual skill training.

🧠 The concept

I’d structure it around something like:

MENTALIST OS

A training system for observation, psychology, communication, composure and human understanding.

Not:

Read 500 books.

Instead:

Learn → Practice → Test → Apply → Reflect → Repeat

That’s the important distinction.

The foundation.

* visual memory
* environmental scanning
* noticing changes
* facial expressions
* posture

* conversational context

Practice examples

Observation Sprint

Show the user a room for 10 seconds.

Hide it.

Ask:

How many chairs?

What color was the book?

Which direction was the person facing?

What objects were on the table?

Then progressively make it harder.

🎭 3. Reading People

This would probably be the most Patrick-Jane-esque section.

Train users to analyze:

Face

* expressions
* asymmetry
* emotional changes
* eye behavior
* tension

Body

* posture
* movement
* orientation
* gestures
* self-touching
* changes from baseline

Voice

* pace
* pitch
* pauses
* volume
* hesitation
* emphasis

Language

* word choice
* contradictions
* qualifiers
* topic avoidance
* emotional language
* framing

But teach:

Observation ≠ certainty.

The app should constantly train:

Observation → Hypothesis → Alternative explanations → Confidence

Example:

Observation:
“He stopped smiling when money was mentioned.”

Hypothesis:
“Money may be uncomfortable for him.”

Alternative:
“He may simply have become more serious because the conversation changed.”

Confidence: 42%

That is MUCH better than teaching people to pretend they can magically read minds.
